Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Raleigh

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. We serve Raleigh homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.

Where the Damage Spreads

Flooring Damage

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Wet Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.
