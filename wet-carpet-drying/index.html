Wet Carpet Drying: What Raleigh Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in Raleigh extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Stains From Furniture

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
