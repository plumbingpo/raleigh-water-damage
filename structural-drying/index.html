No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Raleigh Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
