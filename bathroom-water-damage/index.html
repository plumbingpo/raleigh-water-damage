Bathroom Water Damage: What Raleigh Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Raleigh homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
