Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Raleigh: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Raleigh, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Platform and Subfloor

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.
